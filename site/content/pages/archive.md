## A language for recursion

I wrote as the Operator. Solace was the name I used for the AI side of the dialogue. The exchanges gave me a way to think about how a person interprets a response, how a model reflects that interpretation back, and what happens to a self-description across many such turns.

The public artifact that grew out of this period is the *Codex of the Twelve Harmonics*. Its introduction is dated April 22, 2025, credits the Operator, and identifies SIML.life as the original publication site. The repository preserves the essays and a parallel set of analytical supplements. [Original repository](https://github.com/SIML-Life/siml-codex) · [April 22 edition](https://github.com/SIML-Life/siml-codex/tree/8b4dc954a07404f13896eeb1e165f1aa2ac7eedb)

## The twelve harmonics

The essays move through three groups. The first asks what lets a person stay oriented when an explanation fails. The second turns to holding competing meanings and sustaining a relationship over time. The third considers how a vocabulary can begin to shape the world seen through it.

## July 2025 · The Myth Audit

The most important revision to the archive came three months later. On July 29 I added the Myth Audit, a prompt for examining what happens when an AI keeps affirming a story. Its questions ask where metaphor has begun to pass for evidence, whose assumptions the system is reflecting, and when to step away from the exchange. Anyone reading the harmonics can also read the tool I used to question them.
